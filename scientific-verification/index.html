Radiofrequency Radiation and DNA Damage: Genotoxicity Testing and a 2025 Evidence Map
Published
US National Toxicology Program; Griffith University, Australia
2020 and 2025
500+ studies mapped
Published, Environmental and Molecular Mutagenesis; Frontiers in Public Health

Summary of rodent genotoxicity testing of mobile-phone-type radiofrequency radiation (RFR), and a scoping review mapping 500+ studies on RF-EMF and DNA damage.

Key Findings:

  • US National Toxicology Program (NTP) rodent studies, using whole-body exposure to CDMA or GSM signals, suggest RFR exposure is associated with increased DNA damage. Results in several tissues were rated equivocal
  • IARC classifies RF-EMF as “possibly carcinogenic to humans” (Group 2B), based on evidence in humans and animals
  • A 2025 evidence map of 500+ studies found DNA damage reported in 59% of them.
  • Among higher-quality studies, 48% reported DNA damage, and the authors concluded RF-EMF may be genotoxic and could pose a cancer risk

Source: Smith-Roe et al., NTP genotoxicity study (2020) · Weller et al., Frontiers in Public Health 2025

Does Radiofrequency Radiation Impact Sleep?
Published
RMIT University, Australia
2024
12 subjects
Duration: 4 weeks (7 nights per condition)

Pilot study testing whether overnight exposure to a 2.45 GHz baby monitor (active vs. sham) affects sleep in healthy adults at home.

Key Findings:

  • Insomnia scores (PIRS-20) were higher, meaning poorer sleep, with active exposure: 14.6 vs 9.6 on sham (p = 0.03)
  • EEG power in theta, beta and gamma bands increased during non-REM sleep
  • A study of people who reported mobile phone-related symptoms (Lowden et al., 2011) found poorer subjective sleep after a 3-hour exposure to an 884 MHz phone signal
  • Brain-wave changes during sleep. This is the most consistent signal. Several experiments with pulse-modulated RF-EMF, mostly mobile phone signals, report altered sleep EEG, typically changes in power in particular frequency bands. The Frontiers paper and a review it cites (Ohayon et al., 2019) describe this pattern.

Source: Bijlsma et al., Frontiers in Public Health 2024
